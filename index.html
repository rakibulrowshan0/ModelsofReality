02 · The central proposition

Civilization is an evolving reality architecture.

Every society builds ways to select what matters, what is true, who can interpret it, how memory is stored, and who is allowed to act. These architectures become institutions, identities, economies and systems of power.

03 · Cognitive asymmetry

Power begins when one side can define reality for another.

The central distinction is not “smart people versus less smart people.” It is the unequal distribution of cognitive infrastructure: education, data, institutions, capital, expertise, media, computing and the ability to interpret complexity.

04 · A critical comparison

North / South is a historical relationship, not a hierarchy of human worth.

Environmental pressures, institutions, energy, trade, colonial extraction, disease, geography, technology and political organization interacted differently across regions. The research question is how these conditions produced different modes of collective adaptation.

Adapt to ecological abundance and seasonality

Research caution: these are analytical poles, not descriptions of whole continents. Every region contains both adaptation and transformation, cooperation and domination.

05 · Digitalization

When memory becomes data, the architecture changes.

Digitization lowers the cost of copying, storing and transmitting knowledge. Networks can weaken traditional gatekeepers while simultaneously creating new platform monopolies. The same infrastructure can decentralize information and centralize attention.

Reality compression ↔ decompression

06 · Artificial Intelligence

AI changes the model itself.

Modern AI systems specialize in different forms of perception, reasoning and action. Your research treats them not only as tools, but as emerging cognitive infrastructure that can either compress reality into one authoritative output or decompress it into competing hypotheses, evidence and perspectives.
